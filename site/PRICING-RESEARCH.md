## Comparable and adjacent products

- [Mesh, formerly Clay personal CRM](https://me.sh/pricing): Personal is free for up to 1,000 contacts. Pro is $10/month billed annually, or $20/month billed monthly, for unlimited contacts. Team is $40/seat/month annually or $49 monthly. Useful pattern: a generous personal free tier, then a clear unlimited-contact upgrade.
- [Mesh subscription help](https://library.me.sh/knowledge-base/subscription-plans/): Mesh explains Personal as a free core tier and Pro as the power-networker tier. It also says trials require a card to limit spam and abuse, which is a warning for Venn: requiring payment details too early may hurt network invites.
- [Dex pricing](https://getdex.com/pricing/?ref_post=crm-for-teams): Premium is $12/month, Professional is $20/month, Team is $40/user/month. Premium includes LinkedIn sync up to 2,500 connections, 10 research credits/month, and personal CRM integrations. Useful anchor: relationship management can charge $12-$20, but Venn should feel cheaper because network liquidity matters.
- [ChatGPT Plus help](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6950777-what-is-chatgpt-plus): ChatGPT Plus is $20/month. This is the consumer AI reference price Venn should sit below, because Venn is an add-on experience inside ChatGPT, not a primary model subscription.
- [OpenAI Business pricing](https://openai.com/business/pricing/): ChatGPT Business Standard is listed at $20/user/month annually or $25/user/month monthly. Useful anchor: team/business AI pricing clusters at $20+ per user, too high for Venn's early consumer network wedge.
- [Spotify Premium Family](https://www.spotify.com/us/family/): $21.99/month for up to 6 Premium accounts under one roof. Useful pattern: one payer, multiple independent accounts, "listen together or separately." Venn can borrow the emotional shape without the household restriction.
- [Notion pricing](https://www.notion.com/pricing): Free is $0, Plus is $10/member/month, Business is $20/member/month and includes Notion Agent and AI Meeting Notes. Useful pattern: $10 is accepted for productivity, but per-member pricing is the wrong default for Venn invites.
- [Granola pricing](https://www.granola.ai/pricing): Basic is $0, Business is $14/user/month, Enterprise is $35/user/month. Useful pattern: AI-native productivity apps can sustain a free tier and $10-$20 paid tier when the paid limit is tied to history, advanced AI, and work use.
- [Superhuman Mail pricing](https://superhuman.com/plans/mail): Starter is $25/member/month billed annually; Business is $33/member/month annually. Useful anchor: premium productivity pricing works for high-intensity professional workflows, but would make Venn feel expensive.
- [Dia plans](https://www.diabrowser.com/plans): Browser is free without AI; Better Answers is $10/month; Better Days is $100/month; extra usage credits are $20 blocks. Useful pattern: keep base software free, charge for AI intensity, and avoid surprise overages.
- [Partiful cost FAQ](https://help.partiful.com/en-us/articles/15525599-does-partiful-cost-money): the core platform is free; optional paid features, premium designs, and ticket fees monetize around the free social graph. Useful pattern: social invite products protect the guest experience.
- [Cluely pricing](https://cluely.com/pricing): Starter is free with limited AI responses and meeting notes; Pro is $19.99/month. Useful anchor: AI assistant apps still cluster around $20 for unlimited access.
- [Clay GTM pricing](https://www.clay.com/pricing): Free includes low monthly credits and actions; Launch starts at $185/month; Growth starts at $495/month; AI usage is split between fixed-price and variable token-cost models. Useful warning: two-meter AI pricing is rational for businesses but too complex and anxiety-inducing for Venn consumers.

## PLG, viral pricing, and willingness to pay

- [Menlo Ventures, State of Consumer AI 2026](https://menlovc.com/perspective/2026-the-state-of-consumer-ai/): consumer AI spend tripled to an estimated $40B, 55% of AI users report paying for at least one AI product, typical payers spend $20-$49/month, and the report explicitly advises founders to keep the free tier because it is where users discover value and build habits.
- [a16z Top 100 Gen AI Consumer Apps, 7th edition](https://a16z.com/100-gen-ai-apps-7/): paid consumer AI is concentrated; 4.5% of eligible consumers in YipitData's US e-receipt panel had an active paid subscription to ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude in August 2026, while heavy spenders account for outsize revenue. Useful implication: Venn should avoid charging everyone and instead let high-intent users sponsor the network.
- [Race to Scale growth loops guide](https://www.racetoscale.com/guides/growth-loops-explained): viral loops work when the inviter's product experience improves after invitees join. Useful implication: Venn's paid plan should make inviting feel like the feature, not a tax.
- [Capgemini Consumer Trends 2026 report](https://www.capgemini.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Final-Web-Version-Report-Consumer-Trends-2026.pdf): willingness to pay for chatbot-style automation is lower than usage; the report notes more openness when AI is bundled with other value. Useful implication: price Venn as relationship utility and reciprocity, not abstract AI automation.
- [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2026](https://www-docs.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps/): AI apps increasingly use hybrid monetization and credits, but consumer subscription success still depends on clear perceived value. Useful implication: Venn can defer usage add-ons until power-user behavior is proven.

## Takeaways for Venn

- Venn should anchor below ChatGPT Plus. A Venn subscription above $10/month will be compared to paying for a full model subscription.
- Free cannot mean "demo." For network growth, invited friends must have a durable free role: accept, share, edit, stop sharing, and stay connected.
- The paid unit should be the sponsor, not the invited seat. "I pay so five people I care about never have to" feels generous; "pay per friend" feels punitive.
- Contact limits are familiar. Mesh's 1,000-contact free/paid boundary and Dex's contact-sync limits support using people/contact count as the value metric.
- A patron tier can capture willingness to pay without making the base product feel expensive. It should feel like sponsorship, not artificial feature withholding.
